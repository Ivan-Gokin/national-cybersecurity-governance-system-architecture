## What this is and what it is not

This is a map **at the level of legal acts**: who is a subject of the system, which roles it has been given, on what basis, and how the subjects are linked. It is compiled from open sources only — laws, decrees, resolutions, official releases and a NATO CCDCOE report.

The map deliberately holds no infrastructure objects, systems, networks, addresses or personal names — only bodies and positions. This boundary was set before the first publication and will not change.

It is not legal advice and not the official position of any body. Every row carries a confidence level: "Checked against the act", "From a secondary source" or "Compiler's inference". In the prototype no row has reached the first level yet.

## Where the map comes from

The map grew out of the article "What does Ukraine's national cybersecurity system look like?" (2024, in Ukrainian) and combines two frameworks: the five mandates of the NATO CCDCOE *National Cyber Security Framework Manual* (Klimburg, 2012) and the 20 objectives of ENISA's *National Capabilities Assessment Framework 2.0* (2026). The idea of an open actor map with links is borrowed from the German project [Cybersicherheitsarchitektur](https://www.cybersicherheitsarchitektur.de/) by the think tank interface.

The map and the [GRC foundation](https://ivan-gokin.github.io/grc-foundation-ua/) share one legal base: every role and link cites an act from its register, and the personal card of a class of organisations leads to the requirements that apply to you.

## How to help

An error, a missing actor or link, an act that changed: use "Found an error?" at the bottom of the page or the form on an actor's page. Every change needs a legal basis. How the data is built is in [Method](#/method).
